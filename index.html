Scotiabank

Engineering Data Pipelines & Building an AI Compliance Agent

My dual-track software engineering co-op with Scotiabank Investment Support Services and the Intern Innovation Pod.

Term: Summer 2026 Guelph CS Co-op Toronto, ON

Overview

This term was really split between two different jobs. Tuesday to Friday, I was on Investment Support Services, keeping the systems that portfolio managers rely on accurate and running. But every Monday, that got put on pause for a few hours so I could work with a small pod of other interns building an AI compliance tool from scratch, aimed at speeding up how the bank reviews regulatory documents.

Those two things pulled on completely different muscles. ISS was about precision and not breaking things that already work. The Innovation Pod was about moving fast, building something that didn't exist yet, and being comfortable presenting an unfinished idea to people way more senior than me. Doing both at the same time is really what this report is about.

The Core Team: Investment Support Services

Daily production work

Reporting to Jose Quintero, Senior Manager on ISS and my main reporting manager.

Every single day, Portfolio Managers and other banking people across the bank need a working environment and a workflow they can actually rely on, and that's where my ISS team comes in. We make sure that workflow holds up by building out reports with MS Report Builder, and working across Charles River (CRIMS and CRD), Oracle SQL, and Symcorp environments to keep everything running the way it's supposed to.

Day to day, my main job was querying the Charles River database in Oracle SQL Developer to build the reports Portfolio Managers in other parts of the bank were actually asking for. On top of that I handled a steady stream of support tickets, things like adding new Portfolio Managers onto existing projects in Charles River, or setting up automated emails that announce trades. Doing that kind of work day in and day out is what actually taught me how Scotiabank manages its portfolios and clients, and it forced me to get comfortable talking to people, having to reach out directly to other team members and directors across the bank to understand what they needed before I could help them.

Beyond the day-to-day tickets, my main delivery was architecting and shipping the upcoming maturities report, giving managers clear, real-time visibility into assets that were about to roll over. That one meant sitting with the actual business logic behind portfolio rollover long enough to know I was building the right thing, not just a query that ran without errors.

Tools I used daily

  • Oracle SQL
  • MS SQL Server
  • Charles River CRIMS & CRD
  • MS Report Builder
  • Symcorp

Major Spotlight: The Intern Innovation Pod

Weekly sprint project

Reporting to Laura Bolton (Reporting Manager), Sebastian Blandizzi (VP of Tech), Gautham Pallapa (Director & Product Owner), and Sijad Ghani (Tech Lead).

Every Monday, a small pod of interns dropped our regular team work and became a mini startup for a few hours. The goal was to build an internal AI compliance tool that could help review regulatory documents faster than a human doing it manually. The pace was fast: whatever we committed to at the start of the week, we were expected to demo progress on by the following Monday, no exceptions.

My piece of it was the document intake chunking architecture. Raw compliance and regulatory PDFs are messy: inconsistent formatting, tables mixed into paragraphs, scanned pages, footnotes in weird places. My job was to reliably parse, split, and clean those document streams so they fit cleanly into LLM context windows, without dropping critical clauses and without leaking anything sensitive out of the boundaries we were allowed to work in under our NDA.

We ended up reaching the executive demo stage, presenting a fully working version of the tool to Scotiabank directors and VPs. That was easily the most nerve-wracking fifteen minutes of the term.

Our prototype didn't advance to the final round, even though the feedback was genuinely strong. That stung a bit at first, but it was still one of the best learning experiences I've had, building something real, under real constraints, and defending it in front of people who make decisions that matter.

The 5 Co-op Learning Goals

Straight from Experience Guelph, with the honest version of how each one actually went.

Goal 1 · Tech Literacy

Learn the tools, then ship with them

Plan:

Get functional in Oracle SQL, Charles River, and MS Report Builder fast enough to be useful, not just a spectator on the team.

Reflection:

Mastering Oracle SQL, Charles River, and MS Report Builder on the fly to ship the maturities report and close tickets. There was no ramp-up period, I learned most of these tools by breaking things in a test environment first.

Goal 2 · Teamwork

Own a piece, then defend it

Plan:

Take real ownership of one component in the pod rather than just contributing bits and pieces.

Reflection:

Owning the file ingestion layer within the Monday Innovation Pod and presenting the finished agent to senior executives. Having a piece I fully understood made it a lot easier to answer hard questions live.

Goal 3 · Oral Communication

Talk to people outside my team

Plan:

Stop relying only on my manager and start reaching out directly when tickets touched systems I didn't own.

Reflection:

Breaking out of the intern bubble to collaborate with external IT divisions, clarify data dependencies, and set up informational coffee chats across the bank. Cold-messaging people I'd never met got a lot easier after the first few conversations went fine.

Goal 4 · Critical Thinking

Understand the business, not just the query

Plan:

Learn why the data mattered, not just how to fetch it.

Reflection:

Understanding the actual financial logic behind portfolio rollover so my queries solved real business problems instead of just running without errors. A query that returns fast and clean is worthless if it's answering the wrong question.

Goal 5 · Time Management & Organization

Run two clocks at once

Plan:

Protect Monday pod time without letting ISS tickets pile up during the rest of the week.

Reflection:

Balancing intense, deadline-driven Monday innovation milestones with steady support ticket resolution for the core ISS team. Some weeks this meant just being upfront about what could realistically get done by when, on both sides.

Engineering Takeaways: Theory vs. Enterprise Reality

Guelph classroom

  • Data structures and algorithms, mostly on clean, well-formed input
  • Database normalization taught as a near-universal rule
  • Big-O analysis on isolated problems with no legacy baggage

Enterprise banking

  • Legacy schemas nobody fully documented, some older than my degree
  • Zero tolerance for a corrupted or mismatched number, no "close enough"
  • Working through NDAs and access boundaries that shape what you can even look at

School gives you the theory to reason about a problem in the abstract. Enterprise banking gives you the same problem wrapped in fifteen years of legacy decisions, strict validation requirements, and real consequences if you get it wrong. Both matter, but only one of them teaches you to actually be careful.
